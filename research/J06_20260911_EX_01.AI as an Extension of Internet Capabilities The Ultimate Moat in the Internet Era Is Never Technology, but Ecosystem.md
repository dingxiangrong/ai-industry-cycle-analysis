## Preface
A fundamental cognitive bias has long prevailed in the market: framing AI iteration as ¡°a new round of technological revolution¡±, and equating upgrades in model parameters and reasoning capability with paradigm-level reconstruction comparable to the internet revolution.

From the perspective of underlying industrial order and civilizational evolution, a precise positioning can be established:
The internet represents a paradigm revolution that upends foundational order, while AI is a capability upgrade built atop internet infrastructure and does not qualify as an independent-order revolution.

Ever since the rise of the internet, end-game competition, corporate destinies and divergence of competitive barriers have consistently followed one timeless core axiom:
In the short run, technological iteration dominates; in the medium run, capital scale prevails; in the long run, the only enduring moat lies in ecosystem foundations, not point-in-time technologies.

This axiom directly explains the structural fate of all pure-model AI enterprises.

## I. Paradigm Classification: The Internet as a Spatial Revolution, AI as Value-Adding Information Extension
The sole criterion for defining landmark revolutions in human civilization is whether they break physical-space barriers and reconstruct ground-rules for social collaboration.

The essence of the internet lies in dissolving geographic distance, erasing regional boundaries and building a global real-time connectivity network.
It reshapes the fundamental logic of information flow, commercial transactions, organizational collaboration and resource matching. It constitutes a zero-to-one foundational paradigm revolution that transforms how the world operates.

AI creates no new connectivity logic, breaks no physical-space barriers, nor rebuilds the bedrock of social collaboration.
Its full value is confined to intelligent processing, comprehension, generation and reasoning upgrades applied to pre-existing internet information, datasets and connection networks.

Plain definition:
The internet builds a borderless global information highway network. AI merely represents upgraded intelligent vehicles travelling upon that highway.
The highway itself is the revolution; vehicle improvements are only extended optimization. The two do not occupy the same industrial tier.

This yields a core corollary:
AI possesses no standalone industrial base. The entire AI industry is essentially a branch extension of the internet industry. Discussing an independent AI revolution or self-sufficient AI commercial empire decoupled from internet ecosystems rests on invalid foundational logic.

## II. Evolution of Hardware End-Devices: PC and Mobile Phone ¡ª Three Human-Centered Iterations
The entire evolution of the internet is firmly rooted in a human-centric framework, taking human needs and human activities as its logical starting-point. The evolutionary path unfolds across three revolutionary phases:

### Phase-1: Personal Computer ¡ª Bringing Humans into the Digital World
The PC is not the internet itself; it is an end-user carrier.
Its core function is to connect human beings into the digital realm.
For the first time, individuals gained operable digital terminals. However users were geographically tethered to hardware locations, with fixed-point network access only. Physical-space barriers were only partially loosened, and the full spectrum of human demands remained largely unfulfilled.

### Phase-2: PC-Based Internet Era ¡ª Dismantling Physical-Space Barriers
Early internet built upon PC hardware delivered the first core revolution: neutralizing physical distance.
Social interaction, commerce, knowledge-seeking and communication had previously been constrained by geography. Once networks emerged, people thousands of miles apart could interact instantaneously.

Major limitations persisted: users had to be physically present at a PC to go online. Human life scenarios remained hardware-bound, and the full scope of human demands was not unlocked.

### Phase-3: Mobile Internet ¡ª Dual Integration of Physical Space and Demand-Space
This marks the most powerful leap in internet history.
Demand-space is defined as the complete collection of human intrinsic needs: social communication, learning, entertainment, commerce, productive collaboration and content creation ¡ª the full dimension of human life, mental and operational requirements.

Smartphones carry network connectivity everywhere, freeing users from fixed-location constraints.
On one hand, physical-space separation is further diminished; on the other hand, humanity¡¯s full demand-space is thoroughly unlocked.
Wherever people are, socializing, entertainment, shopping, learning and collaboration can be accomplished online.
The mobile internet achieves full alignment between physical space and human demand-space.

Only once these two dimensions converge does a truly human-centered digital society take shape.
All applications, services and business models grow in response to diverse human demands, giving rise to vast self-sustaining ecosystems.

> **Core Thesis**: PCs ¡°bring humans into the digital world¡±; PC-era internet shatters physical-space constraints; the mobile internet fully unlocks human demand-space. These three steps represent paradigm-shifting iterations.

Hardware carriers are merely entry points. Real competitive victory hinges upon ecosystems rapidly growing on top of them.
Windows secured its PC-era dominance via its software-developer ecosystem; the contest between iOS and Android was ultimately decided by application ecosystems. Hardware craftsmanship and chip technology can be caught up with, yet once mature ecosystems solidify, switching costs become extremely high and disruption becomes difficult.

Within the human-centered framework, the ecosystem forms the foundational bedrock. An end-device without an ecosystem is merely isolated hardware, unable to unlock the internet¡¯s connectivity dividends.

## III. Core Iron Law of the Internet Era: Technology Provides No Long-Term Moat; Ecosystems Deliver Enduring Barriers
In traditional industrial eras, competitive barriers stemmed from manufacturing techniques, patents, specialized equipment and proprietary technical secrets.
In the internet era, discrete technologies become transparent, open-source and replicable.

The AI sector perfectly illustrates this rule:
Underlying large-model architectural principles are fully public. Training workflows, alignment methodologies and algorithm paradigms are shared globally.
So-called technical leadership is not a barrier of original principles, but a temporary engineering advantage built through capital investment.

- Computing power can be purchased with capital,
- Talent can be recruited via competitive compensation,
- Engineering know-how can be replicated through team acquisitions,
- Model capability gaps can be closed within 6-12-months by open-source follow-on development.

Under internet-era conditions of globalization, open academic publication and free talent mobility, no discrete technology can sustain permanent monopolies.
Technology continuously iterates and gets superseded; it cannot serve as an enterprise¡¯s ultimate foundational base.

The sole non-replicable, non-catch-up-able, non-quick-to-build ultimate moat in the internet age is:
**A mature, large-scale, self-cycling ecosystem foundation with high switching costs.**

An ecosystem encompasses four irreplaceable layers of barriers:
1. User-and-traffic ecosystem: established user mindsets, usage habits and full-market coverage;
2. Developer-and-tool ecosystem: plugins, SDKs, engineering pipelines and upstream-downstream compatibility;
3. Business-and-scenario ecosystem: deployment use-cases, domain know-how and legacy-system lock-in;
4. Commercial-and-cash-flow ecosystem: stable recurring revenue, self-sustaining cash generation and self-reinforcing iteration loops.

Technology may be copied; complete ecosystems can never be duplicated.

Building upon core conclusions in *The Ultimate Destiny of Pure-Model AI Enterprises*, the fatal flaw shared by all independent pure-model firms is identical:

Under internet rules where ecosystems reign supreme:
**Entities without ecosystems possess no pricing power, no right to independent survival and no viable long-term destiny.**

This explains why pure-model enterprises can never establish stable standalone market positions. Models represent capabilities only. To continuously realize commercial value, they must be embedded within ecosystems composed of end-devices, real-world scenarios, business workflows and developer communities. Much like PCs or mobile phones whose value diminishes in isolation from internet ecosystems; no matter how capable an AI model may be, without an ecosystem foundation it remains an isolated tool asset, destined for acquisition and absorption by ecosystem owners.

## Conclusion
From personal computers to smartphones and today¡¯s AI wave, the core industrial storyline remains unchanged:
The internet completed a human-centered dual spatial revolution: first connecting humanity into digital space via PCs, then dismantling physical-space constraints, and finally fully unlocking human demand-space.

AI creates no new spatial dimensions. It delivers enhanced information-processing capacity operating within the already-established human-centric digital world.

Discrete hardware, isolated algorithms or standalone models can only deliver transient window-of-opportunity advantages. Rapidly constructing self-cycling ecosystems constitutes the only lasting moat within the internet era.
