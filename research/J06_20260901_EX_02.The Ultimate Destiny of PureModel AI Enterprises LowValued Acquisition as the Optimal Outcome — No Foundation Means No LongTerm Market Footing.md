They fail to identify the structural fate of pure-model AI enterprises. Drawing on real-world M&A and bankruptcy cases from 2025-2026 together with fundamental barriers in enterprise services, it can be confirmed: pure general-purpose large-model firms suffer more than mere over-valuation. Devoid of viable transformation tracks, steady-state cash flow, or durable valuation anchors, these entities face three fated outcomes amid the ongoing AI computing-liquidity cleanup cycle. Among them, low-residual-value acquisition represents the best and most dignified exit path.

## I. Foundational Premise: Pure-Model Firms Can Never Transform into IBM-Style Enterprise Service Providers
A pervasive market misconception holds that OpenAI, Anthropic and comparable general-model startups could pivot toward enterprise-grade services, vertical industry informatization, and AI consulting and delivery once general-purpose API markets saturate. The fantasy envisages them maturing into next-generation technology service providers emulating the century-old commercial moat of IBM or Accenture.

This assumption is fundamentally invalid and betrays misunderstanding of core barriers within enterprise services.

IBM¡¯s century-long resilience in enterprise informatization never stemmed from models, algorithms, or computing power. Its irreplaceable moat rests on three pillars:
1. High-level industry consulting capability: mapping enterprise architectures, redesigning workflows, establishing compliance frameworks, and optimizing organizational efficiency;
2. End-to-end delivery capacity: tens-of-thousands-strong implementation teams for on-site deployment, system integration, and full-lifecycle operation & maintenance guarantees;
3. Century-old government-enterprise relationship capital: accumulated industry know-how, trusted project credentials, and sustained repeat-purchase ecosystems.

Models serve merely as minor tooling components within IBM¡¯s broader solution stack. IBM¡¯s own trajectory in 2026 delivers conclusive corroboration: its market capitalization once surged above $300 billion amid the AI boom. However, as corporate budgets were heavily siphoned toward AI servers and HBM hardware, its Q2 results triggered a 25% single-day share-price crash, pulling market cap back to around $205 billion with a static P/E ratio of only 18x. Even established IT incumbents with complete delivery infrastructure, compliance systems, and long-standing client moats cannot withstand AI-era budget shocks and valuation contractions. Pure-model vendors relying solely on tech narratives and zero industrial foundations stand even less chance of gaining traction within enterprise service markets.

Independent large-model startups are trapped by irreversible genetic constraints. Their organizational structures are optimized exclusively for algorithm iteration, model training, compute orchestration, and API optimization. They possess no consulting divisions, no field-proven delivery workforce, no deep vertical-industry expertise, no experience integrating legacy systems, and no track record serving government-enterprise clients.

These companies excel at building models, not executing projects. Though capable of generating model weights, tuning parameters, and boosting inference throughput, they cannot deliver standard enterprise private deployments, compliance remediation, ERP/MES legacy-system integration, or business-process re-engineering for industry use-cases.

There exists a complete mismatch between technical DNA and industrial-delivery DNA. Transformation is not merely undesirable ¡ª it is functionally infeasible.

This yields the first iron law: **Pure-model enterprises have no viable second-growth curve. They cannot pivot into informatization markets nor evolve into industrial service providers.**

## II. Triple Competitive Squeeze: No Room for Independent Survival for Pure Intermediaries
Per prior research on industrial end-states, only two categories of viable players will persist across the AI industry, sealing off every survival niche for pure-model firms:

**Category 1: Tech Giants with Closed-Loop In-House Capabilities**
Microsoft, Google, ByteDance, Alibaba, Tencent, Amazon. Models become internalized utility infrastructure supporting proprietary end-user products, traffic pools, and native scenarios. Operations do not depend on external clientele or API revenue streams; firms face minimal market-driven competitive pressure and enjoy unbounded iterative capacity. Public-facing API offerings remain spillover side-businesses rather than core strategic priorities.

**Category 2: Incumbent Traditional-IT Players Upgrading Vertical-Industry Informatization**
Deployment spans finance, manufacturing, government, healthcare, and education verticals. Core competitive advantages center on delivery, compliance, adaptability, operation-and-maintenance, and vertical customization.
Key incumbents: Huawei, telecom carriers, Softstone, China National Software, Neusoft, Accenture, Deloitte, IBM.
Valuation framework follows conventional IT-service benchmarks: low gross margins, stable cash-flow profiles, free of trillion-dollar-scale hype narratives. Meanwhile open-source models such as Llama and Qwen satisfy over 90% of vertical-industry requirements, eliminating scenario-specific premium pricing for closed-source general-purpose large models.

These two dominant tracks fully block viable market niches for pure-model companies.

Pure-model firms occupy a classic sandwiched intermediary position:
- Upstream: Unable to compete against closed-loop giants whose internal workflows siphon core B-side demand;
- Downstream: Incapable of penetrating vertical-industry delivery markets due to missing talent, organizational capacity, and domain moats;
- Mid-tier general-purpose API markets face sustained contraction, ruinous price competition, and evaporating gross margins.

Under this three-pronged squeeze, self-sustaining commercial cycles collapse entirely. The pure intermediary business of selling model APIs survives only as a transitional artifact of industrial evolution.

## III. Three Hierarchical Ultimate Outcomes for Pure-Model Firms: Low-Valued Acquisition Represents the Best-Case Scenario
As of September 2026, all foundation-less pure large-model startups face only three foreseeable outcomes over the next 18-30 months, ordered from best-to-worst. All outcomes are substantiated by real-world commercial cases from 2025-2026, representing ongoing industry consolidation rather than hypothetical future projections.

### Best-Case Outcome: Low-Valued Acquisition & Full Team Integration (Ceiling-Level Realistic Exit)
This is the most favorable, dignified, and realistic end-state.

Today¡¯s lofty valuations reflect narrative-driven bubbles rather than intrinsic commercial worth. Once long-term compute liabilities come due, fundraising windows tighten, and growth expectations deflate, sharp valuation corrections are inevitable.

Deprived of organic profitability and feasible transformation pathways or untapped new markets, surviving firms retain value only via core R&D teams, trained model weights, compute-orchestration know-how, and pools of skilled technical talent.

These assets hold negligible standalone market value yet deliver meaningful supplementary value for technology giants and established industrial conglomerates. The predictable result: bubble-inflated valuations are written off, and businesses are acquired at residual technical-asset valuations.

Potential acquirers extend well beyond established tech giants. The largest future buyers will frequently be large non-AI industrial conglomerates: traditional manufacturing champions, financial holding groups, global trading enterprises, and large government-backed industrial platforms. Such entities possess abundant capital, extensive real-world industry scenarios, yet lack in-house AI foundations and R&D teams. They will not pay bubble-era premiums, but will acquire mature technical assets at depressed prices amid market consolidation.

> Empirical Case Study: On August 27, 2026, blockchain infrastructure leader Consensys acquired Sword AI for $300 million. Sword AI exemplified a pure-model tooling venture with finished products, paying customers and operating cash-flow but zero proprietary end-user foundation. The final purchase price decoupled completely from AI-bubble valuations, approximating annual personnel burn plus four-months of runway. It serves as the benchmark for future residual-value fire-sale acquisitions of pure-model firms.

For pure-model enterprises, being acquired at residual value with smooth team integration, orderly investor exits, and preserved technical utility represents the most favorable alternative short of liquidation.

### Sub-Optimal Outcome: Technical Talent Absorption, Hollowed-Out Legal Entity, De Facto Obsolescence
Certain prominent branded entities will avoid formal dissolution yet slip into operational stagnation. Model development halts, go-to-market teams are eliminated, and commercial operations cease entirely. Only brand trademarks plus a skeleton crew of key technical staff are retained, functioning as a technical or patent shell controlled by capital stakeholders.

Entities cease meaningful revenue generation and value creation, devolving into fallback reservoirs for technology, compute capacity and intellectual property. They exhibit zero growth, negligible valuation, and no independent strategic relevance, fading into long-term marginalization.

> Empirical Case Study: In July 2025, legacy SaaS giant ServiceNow purchased Moveworks for $2.8 billion. The enterprise-AI team was fully absorbed within the acquirer¡¯s ecosystem, while the original independent corporate entity lost all strategic purpose and became functionally obsolete.

### Worst-Case Outcome: Zero Acquisition Appeal and Direct Liquidation
Numerous mid-to-tail pure-model startups will confront harsh realities: they hold insufficient value even to attract acquirers.

Widespread model capability homogenization exists; open-source alternatives replicate over 90% of closed-source model functionality. Companies lack exclusive patents, differentiated moats, or privileged compute access. Talent mobility and replicable model weights mean no component qualifies as irreplaceable. Upon market consolidation, residual asset value trends toward zero.

With neither buyers nor stakeholders willing to sustain operations, firms face compute-contract defaults, fundraising collapse, mass staff departures, and corporate liquidation.

> Empirical Case Study: Builder.ai, once valued at $1.5 billion, filed for bankruptcy in 2025 alongside SEC regulatory allegations. Its collapse validates the terminal fate of tail-end pure-model ventures: near-zero residual value, no acquirers, and forced market exit.

## IV. Final Conclusion: The Fundamental End-State of AI Entrepreneurship ¡ª No Scenario Foundation Equals No Independent Destiny
By inception, all pure large-model startups carry an inescapable condition: permanent independent operational control is unattainable. They only possess a finite window for technical arbitrage.

The 18-30-month consolidation timeline is not merely a risk warning, but a countdown to fate:
- Technical competitive advantages persist merely 6-12 months;
- Absence of consulting-delivery capacity blocks vertical-industry penetration;
- Missing native end-user scenarios preclude closed-loop self-sufficiency;
- Heavy fixed compute liabilities eliminate any runway for gradual ¡°muddling through¡±.

The only dignified path forward consists of low-valuation acquisition and full assimilation within giant-enterprise or traditional-industry ecosystems.

A key market misconception must be corrected: the IPO pushes pursued by Anthropic and OpenAI through 2026-2027 do not signal operational profitability or value realization. They constitute balance-sheet debt roll-overs using public capital ahead of maturing long-term compute obligations. IPOs can delay liquidation, equity dilution, or acquisition outcomes by 12-18 months but cannot overturn fundamental industrial axioms.

The future AI landscape will sustain no large, fully independent, market-oriented pure-model corporations. All model assets will ultimately fall into one of two buckets:
- Internalized as infrastructure components controlled by large incumbents,
- Or embedded as modular AI capabilities within traditional vertical-industry informatization solutions.

Standalone commercial intermediaries focused purely on model sales will exit the stage permanently following the bursting of the current bubble.
