## Introduction

The AI industry is in its early rapid-expansion phase, with explosive growth in capital investment and capacity deployment across the whole industrial chain. For global semiconductor enterprises covering AI computing power, advanced process nodes and high-end storage, this is not systematic overvaluation across all asset segments. Instead, we observe premium valuations in high-end tracks alongside profit erosion in mid-and-downstream segments. Markets extrapolate linearly the industry¡¯s early-stage rapid expansion into perpetual long-term growth. This triggers a complete evolutionary process including capital-intensive capacity expansion, supply-demand mismatch, profit restructuring and reshaping of the global industrial landscape. This prospective analysis differentiates capital-market expectation shifts from physical capacity release rhythms, anchoring the critical time window of 2H2027-2029 with the cycle inflection centered in 2028.

## I. Early-Stage High-Growth Creates High-End-Track Premium While Mid-and-Downstream Segments Suffer Erosion

During the early expansion of the AI industry, global computing-power and semiconductor sectors heat up rapidly. Market performance exhibits obvious structural divergence: valuation premiums concentrate narrowly on high-end tracks such as GPU, HBM and advanced packaging. Mature-process nodes, consumer electronics, MCUs and other traditional segments remain stuck in cyclical troughs without broad-based valuation bubbles.

Upstream leaders capture scarcity-driven order premiums: TSMC¡¯s 2025 capital expenditure reached USD 40.5-billion, with 2026 guidance of USD 52-56-billion, hitting an all-time high. SK Hynix¡¯s projected 2026 capital expenditure reaches 40-trillion KRW, equivalent to roughly 35-% of revenue, with core HBM orders booked through 2027. Meanwhile, mid-and-downstream segments already show early signs of cooling, forming a typical pattern: ¡°upstream prints money, downstream bleeds profits¡±. For instance, Samsung Electronics recorded record-high operating profit in 2Q2026, yet its stock price dropped 7-% upon earnings release. Markets priced in cyclical downturn in advance, interpreting current peak profits as near-term highs, with P/E ratio held at merely 20¡Á and cyclical expectations already weakening.

The industrial value-restructuring logic can be clearly observed from hardware cost breakdown: for a single B200 chip, HBM memory accounts for 42-%, CoWoS plus associated testing accounts for 21-%, and advanced-process manufacturing accounts for only 23-%. Five years ago, packaging represented less than 10-% of total chip cost. Today storage plus packaging exceed 60-% of total cost. Manufacturing has evolved from an absolute leading role to merely one of three core segments, with value capture nearly halved. This represents a long-term trend. Between 2026-2027, profit migration away from the two core pools remains in early stages and is not yet fully realized.

In the short run, profit migration is incomplete and the industry stays at the tail end of the boom: NVIDIA maintains 75-%+ gross margins, and large-purchase agreements lock capacity for the next two years. TSMC¡¯s CoWoS order book extends into 2027, still operating in a seller¡¯s market with capacity scarcity. Large-scale profit exodus from manufacturing and full cyclical reversal will materialize precisely during 2H2027-2029, when overseas capacity floods hit and heavy-asset depreciation fully bites, with the cycle inflection in 2028.

## II. Overseas Giants Are Swept by Market Momentum and Enter a Positive Heavy-Asset Expansion Cycle

Driven by industry euphoria, downstream long-term order commitments and peer capacity-building arms races, overseas top giants show divergent expansion behavior. Not all are trapped in irreversible heavy-asset paths.

Benefiting from robust customer stickiness and steady gross margins of 59-63-%, TSMC maintains controllable financial leverage. Its capacity expansion responds to genuine demand pull and boasts the strongest cyclical resilience; it is not a passive hostage to heavy-asset burdens. SK Hynix and Samsung tie their HBM businesses heavily to a small set of core clients, with capital-expenditure-to-revenue ratios of 28-35-%. They fall into a passive profit trap: higher earnings trigger larger reinvestment. Highly specialized production lines cannot be repurposed, and exit costs are extremely high, locking them firmly into positive expansion loops and rendering them the most vulnerable participants in this cycle. Micron launches multibillion-dollar factory construction in the United States yet holds weaker market share and customer bargaining power, leaving it most exposed to operational pressure once the cycle reverses.

In general, high-end semiconductor fabs are highly specialized. Their 2-3-year construction and ramp-up cycles are irreversible and cannot be paused halfway. Some overseas enterprises ramp capital spending at cyclical peaks, taking on massive rigid heavy-asset burdens and leverage debt and sowing seeds for subsequent cyclical deterioration and profit blow-back.

Profit migration exhibits asymmetric structural characteristics: the advanced intermediate-packaging pool delivers long-term sustainable structural dividends driven by post-Moore¡¯s-Law technological evolution. The computing-electricity synergy pool represents local dividends unique to China, deeply tied to domestic land and power-grid resources. By contrast, the HBM storage pool features extreme cyclicality and the highest risk, and will be where reverse-cycle stress emerges first. As a direct downstream extension of the high-end logic foundry pool, HBM storage moves in lockstep with top-tier computing-power expansion cycles, delivering the largest boom-time elasticity yet suffering the swiftest blow-back amid oversupply. This fully aligns with the core definition that every profit pool carries explicit failure boundaries.

## III. High-End Computing-Power Increment Continues Cooling: Three-Layer Hard Constraints Lock Industry-Growth Inflection

The overall AI industry keeps growing, yet the era of exponential growth for high-end computing power has ended. Incremental momentum cools under three mutually reinforcing hard constraints.

First, high-end computing-power demand has natural upper bounds. Consumer-facing everyday-AI applications and AI-tool iteration form the fundamental demand base. Enterprise-Agent inference, end-side-AI devices and autonomous-driving closed-loop training open secondary growth curves. Multiple institutions designate 2026 as ¡°the Year of Agent Inference¡±. Short-term demand has not peaked, yet long-term incremental space has clear limits and cannot sustain perpetual exponential expansion.

Second, software-driven technical iteration delivers sustained computing-power reduction. Treat computing-power consumption as core operational cost for the AI industry. Through model distillation, quantization-pruning, inference-cache optimization, sparse-architecture iteration, MoE and other techniques, hardware consumption of high-end computing and HBM memory drops by 30-40-% annually for equivalent business volume and output quality. Counterbalancing model-parameter scaling, high-end computing-power growth decelerates from exponential to a steady 30-45-% compound annual growth rate, closely matching TSMC¡¯s 45-% AI-revenue growth guidance for 2024-2029.

Last but critically important: extreme demand segmentation appears across the industry. This is the most essential yet easily overlooked logic of current industrial transformation. Not all AI workloads require top-tier high-end computing power. Mass everyday-inference tasks, lightweight-AI applications and edge-end scenarios can be adequately served by mid-tier, edge or general-purpose computing resources. Only ultra-large-model training and ultra-complex inference require cutting-edge process nodes and premium HBM. High-end computing power ceases to be a universal requirement for all AI work; stratified computing and scenario-specific resource allocation become industry norms.

Combined, these three constraints diminish high-end-track high-growth dividends and stabilize deceleration gradients for industry increments. Supply-demand and boom-bust inflection points will land precisely within 2H2027-2029, with cycle inflection centered on 2028.

## IV. Capital-Market Expectations Move First: Phased Valuation-Reset and Reverse-Deterioration Cycle Initiate

Capital markets price future expectations rather than current-period financial results; valuation adjustments always precede real-economy shifts. Clear inflection signals emerged as early as July-2026: Morgan Stanley warned of significant overbuying in high-end semiconductor tracks, forecasting 30-40-% potential downside for select AI-chip equities. Following Samsung earnings releases, valuation de-rating took place rapidly, and multiple AI-concept stocks corrected more than 40-% within a single month. Industry-expectation re-evaluation has already commenced.

Valuation corrections transmit sequentially from periphery to core: mid-and-downstream cloud vendors and AI-concept stocks adjust first. Core hardware players such as TSMC and SK Hynix remain sheltered from deep re-rating thanks to order visibility extending into 2027. The core thesis that ¡°markets will complete pricing corrections within roughly one year¡± remains valid, but adjustments unfold in phases rather than as simultaneous sector-wide collapse.

Valuation contraction can trigger vicious reverse-industry cycles: sharp asset-valuation write-downs reduce collateral-based financing capacity, raise refinancing difficulty and push up market funding costs. Leverage-debt risks accumulated from prior capacity expansion amplify. Heightened debt pressure further erodes market sentiment and compresses asset valuations in a self-reinforcing negative feedback loop that can escalate into material debt crises. Consequently, the prior positive heavy-asset expansion cycle of overseas giants reverses into a deterioration cycle.

## V. Severe Capacity-Cycle Mismatch: High-End-Capacity Flood in 2H2027-2029, Inflection in 2028

During the industry boom, massive high-end computing-power, HBM and advanced-packaging capacity projects broke ground worldwide. Semiconductor fabs follow 2-3-year construction-and-ramp timelines; physical capacity behaves as a slow variable. Once construction commences, projects cannot be halted midway, creating severe supply-demand cyclical mismatches.

Key capacity milestones are concrete: CoWoS advanced-packaging capacity rises from 70-k wafers/month at end-2025 to 120-k by end-2026 and 200-k by end-2027. The three global memory giants allocate 70-% of new capacity to the HBM track. The 2026 HBM market size grows 58-% year-on-year to USD 54.6-billion. The genuine capacity-surplus flood arrives during 2H2027-2029, with the cycle inflection centered in 2028, when large-scale Korean memory clusters and Micron¡¯s new U.S. fabs fully ramp. High-end computing power, HBM and advanced packaging will then face structural capacity surplus.

Cyclical mismatch creates dual profit-squeezing pressure on prices and costs: oversupply pushes product prices lower. Meanwhile insufficient order volume reduces fab-utilization rates. Rigid depreciation and operating expenses are allocated across fewer units, lifting per-unit manufacturing costs passively. Falling selling prices paired with rising unit costs compress profit margins and set the stage for industry-wide profit collapse and stress for leading players.

## VI. Rigid Depreciation Delivers Two-Way Blow-Back: Systemic Industry-Profit Collapse and Risk Stratified Transmission

High-end semiconductor manufacturing is heavy-asset intensive. Depreciation of plant and equipment, fab operation and maintenance, and specialized-labor costs represent rigid fixed expenditures independent of order volume and cannot be flexibly cut back, creating powerful profit blow-back during cyclical downturns.

HBM high-end production lines are highly specialized and carry heavy depreciation burdens. When capacity utilization falls from a boom-time 95-% to roughly 70-%, per-unit manufacturing costs surge. Combined with falling selling prices, extreme two-way profit compression occurs. Memory players such as SK Hynix, saddled with 35-% capital-expenditure-to-revenue ratios, display highly fragile profit elasticity. Once product prices soften, profits can collapse rapidly, making memory vendors the segment where reverse-cycle stress manifests first.

Industry risks follow a clear transmission sequence: HBM memory vendors bear pressure first, followed by IDM logic manufacturers, while pure foundries show the greatest resilience and are impacted last. Supported by diversified customer bases and >60-% gross margins, TSMC can absorb depreciation pressure over long periods and will not suffer initial profit collapse.

Overall, excess profits accumulated during the industry boom get rapidly eroded under triple pressures: product-price declines, rising unit manufacturing costs and higher financing costs. Sharp earnings deterioration among market leaders reinforces reverse-deterioration loops and accelerates downward pressure on overseas heavy-asset-burdened giants.

## VII. Sino-U.S. Technological-Gap Creates Natural Risk-Avoidance Advantage; Sanctions Force Accumulation of Catch-Up Capacity

A 2-3-year technological gap separates Chinese and U.S. semiconductor and AI-computing industries, analogous to runners on a racetrack with China positioned one lap behind. This objective gap meant China avoided participating in high-leverage, high-risk heavy-asset investment during the global high-end-capacity boom of 2023-2025. China avoided building massive depreciation liabilities and sidestepped the overseas high-end-capacity-surplus reservoir of risk.

This industrial corner-overtaking is not a technology-track switch but a cyclical-downturn corner within the same AI-computing-power semiconductor track. Overseas front-runners took on heavy-asset leverage during boom peaks and must deleverage under duress as cycles turn. China benefits from its lag position with lighter-asset footing. U.S. export-control measures further force domestic industrial self-reliance, building substantial catch-up capacity to narrow technological gaps at cyclical troughs. Korea¡¯s DRAM industry delivered three rounds of counter-cyclical catch-up in 1986, 1997 and 2008, offering historical validation of this cyclical logic.

Sustained tightening of U.S. export controls forces domestic industries toward full self-reliance, building core capacity across demand, manufacturing, packaging and algorithm dimensions:
1. **Demand Pool**: Curtailed overseas high-end-chip supplies open annual demand for millions of domestic AI accelerators, giving local chip-makers access to large-customer long-term contracts to amortize R&D costs.
2. **Manufacturing Pool**: Stable mass production of SMIC¡¯s N+2 process; N+3 (5-nm-equivalent) process ready mid-2026. Rising global market share for ChangXin Memory and Yangtze Memory. Domestic HBM completes sample delivery and initiates small-batch trial production.
3. **Packaging-Interconnect Pool**: Growing global share for domestic advanced-packaging capacity. Home-grown CoWoS-like processes, super-node clusters and high-speed optical modules achieve global-leading performance.
4. **Algorithm-Optimization Pool**: Domestic large-language models reduce per-token computing consumption via distillation and quantization, offsetting hardware-generation gaps with software advantages.

Critical boundary constraints apply: advantages are not unconditional. Domestic AI chip design, advanced-process nodes, EUV equipment, high-end EDA and software ecosystems still face 1.5-3-year gaps. Risk avoidance via lighter-asset positioning forms a prerequisite; industrial catch-up and landscape breakthrough arise from the combined effects of forced sanctions, home-market backing and system-level innovation, not purely passive dividends.

## VIII. Overseas Cyclical Deleveraging Creates Counter-Cyclical Catch-Up Window for China

Geopolitical and regulatory constraints prevent overseas leaders from transferring physical high-end foundry or core HBM capacity to China. During this cyclical downturn, China receives not physical overseas capacity but accelerated domestic-substitution momentum enabled by global technology spillovers. Three concrete channels: discounted circulation of used overseas equipment, return of high-skilled industry talent, and partial loosening of IP-licensing conditions. Global cloud vendors actively source domestic AI chips to build heterogeneous-computing clusters to mitigate supply-chain risks and cut computing costs. Iteration of domestic storage and computing chips proceeds via both external procurement and indigenous R&D toward closed-loop industrial autonomy.

Once overseas giants complete capacity ramp-ups, unavoidable lifetime rigid-depreciation burdens force them to operate fabs at high utilization even at thin margins to amortize fixed costs. They offer capacity and manufacturing services at discounted prices, further depressing global industry pricing. Under such conditions, China escapes heavy-asset cyclical risks. Building on previously accumulated industrial capacity, China steadily advances technology iteration, yield improvement and ecosystem maturity. Within this window where overseas industries labor under heavy burdens, China narrows technological gaps and pursues reverse breakthroughs in global-industry competition.

The core nature of this domestic opportunity: it is not passive arbitrage from risk-avoidance. Instead, based on cyclical positional gaps, driven by self-reliance and enabled by cyclical corners, China evolves from industrial follower toward narrowing of technological disparities.

## IX. China¡¯s AI-Industry Response Strategy: Accumulate Capacity via Cost Reduction and Maintain Disciplined Follow-Through

Previous reasoning lays out overseas heavy-asset-trap risks, China¡¯s gap-enabled risk-avoidance advantages and corner-overtaking opportunities. Passive risk avoidance constitutes merely a baseline prerequisite. Capturing the 2026-2029 golden window to narrow technological gaps hinges on proactive strategic choices. China¡¯s core guiding strategy for AI and semiconductor industries can be summarized in eight-character principle: **Accumulate capacity via cost reduction; maintain disciplined follow-through**.

Capacity-building represents the strategic core and follows strict priority ordering: take demand segmentation as principal axis and software-driven cost reduction as lever. Demand segmentation establishes structural prerequisites explaining why China should not jump into overseas top-tier-computing-power heavy-asset traps and defines future order and capacity battlegrounds. Software-driven cost reduction serves as engineering implementation tools enabling domestic computing systems to take on, run and profitably serve segmented mid-and-lower-tier workloads. The two are not parallel: segmentation defines the landscape; cost reduction enables implementation. Segmentation carries far greater importance.

### (1) Core of Capacity-Building: Demand Segmentation as Principal Axis ¡ª Structural Risk Avoidance and Redefining Domestic-Computing Battlegrounds

AI-computing demand is not homogeneous and does not universally require state-of-the-art process nodes and ultra-spec HBM. Real-world scenarios exhibit clear stratification, overturning the oversimplified notion ¡°AI equals top-tier high-end computing power¡±. This segmentation forms the most fundamental structural advantage for China¡¯s light-asset corner-overtaking, outweighing raw technological gaps.

**Tier-1: Top-Tier Training Layer (Shrinking share)**. Only iteration of ultra-large-scale foundation models and very few ultra-complex-inference scenarios demand B200-class high-end GPUs, ultra-stacked HBM, cutting-edge process nodes and CoWoS packaging. This track is where overseas giants deploy heavy-assets and high leverage, and where future capacity surplus, depreciation blow-back and price collapse risks concentrate. Its market volume is limited and shrinking. IDC data shows inference workloads will represent 76.8-% of future AI-infrastructure markets while training-workload share keeps contracting, with the training-to-inference computing-consumption ratio gradually moving toward 1:3. Domestic industries should avoid large-scale commitment to this track and refrain from joining top-tier arms races as a matter of structural risk avoidance.

**Tier-2: Scaled-Inference Layer (Domestic core fundamental market)**. Government affairs, finance, industry, enterprise knowledge bases, recommendation-advertising-search pipelines, intelligent customer service and other commercial scenarios feature high concurrency, strong cost sensitivity, low-latency requirements and large-scale deployment. Such workloads do not need flagship-grade GPUs; they can be adequately handled by domestic AI accelerators built on SMIC¡¯s 7-nm-equivalent processes plus conventional HBM3. This represents the primary battleground for future domestic-chip substitution, where domestic AI chips already capture nearly 90-% of China¡¯s domestic market share. Demand here is stable, long-cycle, deployable at scale and enables amortization of R&D and manufacturing costs to build healthy commercial closed loops.

**Tier-3: Edge-and-End-Side-AI Layer (Largest incremental segment, fully differentiated competition)**. End-side devices including mobile phones, PCs, automotive-on-board systems, surveillance and embodied-intelligence hardware enter the ¡°Year of End-Side-AI Deployment¡± in 2026 with fast-rising penetration. Workloads are handled by end-side NPUs and heavily quantized small-models with low per-device computing requirements. This track does not compete head-to-head against overseas high-end GPUs, advanced-process nodes or ultra-spec HBM and represents natural incremental ground for domestic computing hardware.

Strategic value of demand segmentation upgrades the earlier ¡°gap-enabled risk-avoidance¡± logic: overseas heavy-asset leverage, capacity reservoir risks and cyclical losses concentrate within the shrinking top-tier-training layer. Meanwhile domestic industries build, iterate and scale within the expanding scaled-inference plus end-side-AI layers. China avoids heavy-asset traps not merely due to being a late runner but because the intrinsic demand-structure of the industry removes the need to enter top-tier arms races. Massive local scenarios create captive domestic demand. Long-term stable orders from ByteDance, government agencies, energy and manufacturing sectors feed iterative improvements in domestic chips and models, creating a positive closed-loop: real-world workload ¡ú product iteration ¡ú volume scaling.

### (2) Enabler for Capacity-Building: Software-Driven Cost Reduction as Lever ¡ª Making Non-Flagship Computing ¡°Usable, Affordable and Commercially Viable¡±

Demand segmentation redefines battlegrounds. Yet without software-enabled optimization, mid-and-lower-tier computing cannot substitute high-end GPUs. Software-driven cost reduction¡¯s core value is not simple expense cutting but lifting the practical usability of domestic 7-nm-equivalent processes, conventional HBM and home-grown NPUs above commercial-profit thresholds, so that large mid-and-lower-tier markets opened by demand segmentation can be effectively served by domestic computing stacks.

- **Model Layer**: MoE sparse activation, model distillation and parameter pruning deliver extreme resource reduction. DeepSeek-V3 achieves performance comparable to hundred-million-dollar closed-source models with less than USD-6-million training cost, drastically lowering large-model deployment barriers. End-side distillation compresses 70B-class-model capability into 7B-scale models fitting end-device computing limits.
- **Inference Layer**: vLLM continuous batching, PagedAttention, speculative decoding and INT8/INT4 quantization lift GPU utilization from 30-40-% to 70-80-%, cut memory overhead by factor 2-4, reduce inference latency and enable high-cost-performance deployment under controlled precision loss.
- **System Layer**: Domestic advanced packaging, hybrid bonding and Chiplet architectures compensate for single-die-process gaps via multi-chip assembly and super-node interconnection. Huawei CloudMatrix-384 super-node exemplifies how system-level integration offsets single-hardware-unit shortcomings.
- **Deployment Layer**: Full cloud-edge-end three-tier offloading. Central-cloud resources retain only top-tier training and core shared inference; massive latency-sensitive scaled workloads shift to edge and end-side hardware, conserving high-end-computing resources and assigning appropriate workloads across domestic stratified-computing resources.

Through this complete software-optimization stack, per-token costs across the industry drop by orders-of-magnitude within three years. Domestic mid-and-lower-tier computing evolves from ¡°merely functional¡± to ¡°usable, affordable and commercially scalable¡±, delivering engineering support for the strategic implementation of demand segmentation.

### (3) Outcome of Capacity-Building: Full Implementation of the Four Accumulation Pools

Guided by the core logic ¡°demand segmentation as principal, software-driven cost reduction as auxiliary¡±, domestic industrial capacity-building precisely lands within four accumulation pools (China-specific positioning framework) to build comprehensive catch-up strength:

- **Demand Pool**: Forgo pursuit of top-tier scarcity premiums. Capture 76.8-% of inference-market volume plus massive end-side-AI increments and build fundamental market positions via stable local long-term contracts.
- **Manufacturing Pool**: Focus on yield-ramp-up for N+2/N+3 equivalent processes, small-batch iterative testing of domestic HBM and optimization of Chiplet assembly flows. Deepen mid-tier main-track capabilities and avoid top-tier specialized heavy-asset traps.
- **Ecosystem Pool**: Promote deployment of CANN and home-grown inference frameworks. Push leading domestic large-model developers to fully adapt domestic hardware-software stacks and reduce CUDA-ecosystem dependency.
- **Business-Capacity Pool**: Secure stable cash flows through 3-5-year long-term contracts with government-enterprise clients, cloud providers and real-economy industries. Rely on genuine industrial demand rather than capital-market hype to solidify iteration rhythms.

### (4) Disciplined Follow-Through: Avoid Over-Reach and Prevent China-Style Capital Bubbles

Cyclical corners carry high risks of impetuous capacity chasing. Disciplined follow-through is not about backwardness but respecting industrial laws, maintaining optimal catch-up rhythms and avoiding overseas giants¡¯ cyclical fate.

First, adhere to rhythms and avoid over-reaching to fully exploit the capacity-building window. 2026-2027 forms a special ¡°strong-orders yet cooling-expectations¡± torn-period. Overseas capacity floods and depreciation blow-back have not yet materialized; this represents China¡¯s final prime capacity-building window. Strategy priorities: steadily improve yields, ecosystem maturity, customer long-term contracts and supply-chain closure. Resist blind investment in top-tier heavy-asset fabs, avoid premature rigid-depreciation and debt burdens, and refrain from absorbing overseas cyclical-risk reservoirs.

Second, clarify boundaries between follow-through and leadership and allocate resources precisely. Adopt differentiated strategy of ¡°targeted breakthroughs and orderly follow-through¡±: concentrate capital and technical resources to capture global market share in tracks where China holds advantages and can capture cyclical profit migration, including advanced intermediate packaging, computing-electricity synergy, cluster-system design, algorithm optimization and domestic HBM closed-loop development. For cutting-edge single-die process nodes, EUV lithography and high-end EDA where large near-term gaps persist, make steady progress via equivalent-process and system-level compensation. Avoid arms races disconnected from industrial realities; maximize return on limited resources.

Third, resist short-term capital temptations and uphold prudent-operation principles. Valuation divergence and short-term speculative windfalls can induce blind capacity expansion and leverage buildup that replicate overseas ¡°market-swept¡± positive-expansion traps. Domestic industries must preserve strategic discipline. Reject treating temporary scarcity-premium profits as perpetual income. Better to move slower while consolidating asset quality than to rush ahead and take on long-term risks, avoiding repeating overseas-giant cyclical blow-back.

### (5) Strategy Summary

China¡¯s corner-overtaking approach for the AI industry follows clear prioritized logic: demand segmentation forms the strategic root cause, redefining industrial battlegrounds and intrinsically shielding China from overseas heavy-asset traps.
