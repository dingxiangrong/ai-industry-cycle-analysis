## Abstract

This paper puts forward the pseudo-prosperity axiom. It defines the current AI industry as a non-steady-state pseudo-prosperity sustained by capital infusion rather than self-sustaining end-user profitability. Industry survival is constrained by two coupled inflection points: the end-user demand inflection point and the capital-investment-cycle inflection point. End-user demand is decomposed into a three-dimensional system: C-end consumer demand, B-end industrial procurement, and enterprise endogenous productivity transformation. This paper distinguishes individual enterprise resilience gains from the industry-wide steady-state inflection point and provides judgment criteria for inflection points in each dimension.

A four-variable capital-gap model is constructed, consisting of net inflow of new financial capital, rigid industrial consumption, real external operating revenue, and capital outflow from cashing out existing positions. It differentiates behavioral patterns, exit paths and recession rhythms between industrial capital and financial capital, deduces boundary conditions for the continuation and destabilization of pseudo-prosperity, and identifies high-level position cashing-out as an early warning signal. Observable external phenomena corresponding to the two categories of inflection points are summarized. It is emphasized that phenomena serve only as clues; inflection-point judgment must return to axioms and variable models.

Temporal and causal mapping with the parent document¡¯s capacity-cycle analysis is completed. ## Chapter 1 Analysis of Ultimate Core Contradiction: Non-Steady-State Pseudo-Prosperity in the AI Industry

The current AI and supporting hardware industrial chain exhibits prosperous appearances featuring high investment, high valuation and high market enthusiasm. This prosperity is built upon continuous capital inflows instead of steady profits from end-user businesses; it constitutes non-steady-state pseudo-prosperity.

**Axiom 1**: Industry-wide end-user commercial returns cannot cover total consumption across the full industrial chain. Industry prosperity sustained by continuous external capital infusion is defined as pseudo-prosperity. Pseudo-prosperity lacks self-sustaining capability and is constrained by two core inflection points: the end-user demand inflection point and the capital-investment-cycle inflection point.

### 1.1 Two Manifestations of Pseudo-Prosperity

1. **Industrial-side**: Computing-power procurement, R&D expansion and workforce growth drive upstream hardware orders and book-revenue increases. Nevertheless, commercial returns from C-end and B-end end-users fail to cover comprehensive industrial consumption.
2. **Financial-side**: Income circulation within the industry relies on financing, equity transfer and valuation appreciation. Capital returns stem from subsequent capital take-overs rather than operating profits generated by paying customers.

### 1.2 Definition of Two Core Inflection Points

1. **End-User Demand Inflection Point**: Large-scale effective demand and value closed-loops are formed across C-end, B-end and enterprise-endogenous dimensions. The industry develops self-profit-generating capacity, reduces dependence on external capital, and pseudo-prosperity transitions toward steady-state prosperity.
2. **Capital-Investment-Cycle Inflection Point**: The external capital underpinning system fails. New incoming capital cannot offset net industrial consumption; capital investment contracts and the pseudo-prosperity system collapses.

The two inflection points are mutually coupled. End-user demand determines underlying industrial steady-state conditions, whereas capital cycles govern short-term industry survival rhythms.

### 1.3 Constraints for This Chapter

1. Industry maturity shall not be judged merely by order volume, enterprise valuation or public sentiment.
2. All deductions throughout this paper take Axiom-1 as the sole underlying premise.

## Chapter 2 The Real End-User-Demand Inflection Point: Three-Dimensional End-User-Demand System and Divergent Enterprise Pathways

End-user demand encompasses three objective dimensions: external C-end consumer demand, external B-end industrial-procurement demand, and enterprise endogenous productivity-transformation demand.

Divergent viable demand pathways naturally emerge for different enterprises, conditioned by core-business endowments, cash-flow structures and ecosystem positioning. A single entity may cover one or multiple dimensions; the breadth of covered pathways directly determines cyclical resilience.

### 2.1 External C-End Consumer Demand

#### 2.1.1 Migration within Established Tracks

Core traffic for generative-AI today originates from demand diverted from traditional search engines. Three rigid constraints apply: factual-reliability constraints, commercial-paradigm fragmentation constraints, and incumbent-giant ecosystem-defense constraints. Redirected legacy traffic can rapidly expand user bases yet fails to deliver effective paid conversion and cannot sustain steady-state commercialization.

#### 2.1.2 Status of Incremental C-End Scenarios

Incremental productivity-oriented AI scenarios have not become universal public necessities. Products suffer severe homogenization, limited implementation efficiency and heavy reliance on human fallback support. Overall paid-value-for-money has not met user thresholds and cannot support large-scale subscription-based revenue.

#### 2.1.3 C-End Inflection-Point Judgment Criteria

1. Incremental productivity-focused scenarios become primary revenue sources, breaking dependence on diverted search-engine traffic.
2. Stable paid and repurchasing commercial closed-loops are established without subsidy dependence.
3. User-base growth aligns positively and synchronously with revenue growth.

### 2.2 External B-End Industrial-Procurement Demand

#### 2.2.1 B-End Current Situation

Numerous superficial proof-of-concept (POC) pilot projects exist within the industry. These are not embedded into core enterprise operational workflows. Most constitute short-term demonstration-oriented orders driven by capital budgets rather than sustained operational procurement.

#### 2.2.2 B-End Inflection-Point Judgment Criteria

1. AI capabilities are deeply embedded within external enterprises¡¯ core business workflows.
2. Implementation delivers quantifiable cost reduction or revenue growth.
3. Procurement funding originates from enterprise operational budgets and regular repeat purchasing takes shape.

### 2.3 Enterprise Endogenous Productivity-Transformation Demand

Enterprises deploy AI as an internal production tool to reshape existing business workflows, realizing cost reduction, efficiency gains and profit improvements for core operations. This demand generates no standalone external AI revenue; value is internalized within parent-company financial statements while stably driving upstream computing-power and model procurement.

#### 2.3.1 Endogenous-Demand Judgment Criteria

1. AI is deeply embedded within core internal operational workflows instead of being deployed for experimental purposes.
2. Quantifiable improvements in manpower efficiency, turnover ratios and loss reduction are achieved.
3. Computing-power and model procurement is funded from core-business operating budgets.

### 2.4 Pathway Divergence and Resilience Rules

1. Single-pathway implementation: Limited risk buffers, low cyclical resilience.
2. Dual-pathway combined implementation: Risk-hedging capability emerges, moderate resilience.
3. Full implementation across all three pathways: Multi-dimensional demand mutually hedges risks; the entity achieves maximum risk-resistance capacity.

### 2.5 Overall Judgment Rules for the End-User Inflection Point

1. **Industry-Wide Inflection Point**: Large-scale commercial closed-loops must be completed for at least one of the external C-end or B-end dimensions. Industry-wide endogenous efficiency improvement alone cannot sustain overall industrial steady-state conditions, though upstream orders generated by endogenous improvements still count as genuine industrial-chain demand.
2. **Individual-Enterprise Steady-State**: Closed-loop implementation within any single or multiple demand dimensions enhances individual survivability resilience, which does not signify arrival of the industry-wide inflection point.

### 2.6 Constraints for This Chapter

Strictly distinguish individual-enterprise resilience gains from the industry-wide steady-state inflection point. User scale, pilot projects and enterprise endogenous efficiency gains shall not be equated with achievement of the industry-wide end-user inflection point.

## Chapter 3 Capital-Investment-Cycle Inflection Point: Steady-State Collapse of the Financial Underpinning System

Current prosperity across the AI industrial chain fully relies on capital infusion; insufficient end-user profit generation constitutes the industry¡¯s long-term fundamental reality. Collapse of capital underpinning represents the sole financial root logic behind industry inflection points and systemic adjustments.

### 3.1 Dual-Subject Capital Structure

#### 3.1.1 Industrial Capital

Represented by industry incumbents, leading cloud vendors and core industrial-chain enterprises. These entities obtain cross-segment financial support from mature parent-business operations. They feature long fault-tolerance cycles and gradual contraction without risk of instantaneous collapse. Investment contraction occurs only over the medium-to-long term when parent-company profitability deteriorates while AI continues incurring large-scale losses.

#### 3.1.2 Financial Capital

Represented by VC/PE funds, public and private equity vehicles, and secondary-market capital. Driven purely by financial arbitrage without industrial fallback capacity. Survival depends on capital relay closed-loops. Once expectations deteriorate, capital rapidly retreats, financing freezes and panic-driven exits occur.

### 3.2 Two Capital-Exit Logics

1. **Operational Exit**: Real industrial profits are generated via genuine external C-end / B-end revenue to form self-sustaining steady-state closed-loops.
2. **Relay-Based Exit**: No genuine industrial-profit support exists. Arbitrage is completed through subsequent capital take-overs, constituting capital-churning pseudo-closed-loops.

### 3.3 Core Variable Definitions

1. A: Period-specific new inflow of financial capital
2. B: Sustained rigid industrial-wide consumption (computing power, R&D, manpower, depreciation, operating losses)
3. R: Real external AI operating revenue (C-end plus B-end external revenue; endogenous parent-business gains excluded)
4. S: Outflow from cashing-out of high-level existing capital positions (latent industry variable, early-warning signal)

### 3.4 Core Axiomatic Formula

> Net External Capital Gap = (B + S) - R

This gap represents the rigid capital shortfall that must be covered by incoming new financial capital for each operating period.

### 3.5 Dual-Curve Inflection-Point Judgment Model

1. **Steady-State Continuation**: A ¡Ý (B + S - R). Incoming capital fully covers industrial consumption plus position cashing-out; pseudo-prosperity persists.
2. **Inflection-Point Destabilization**: A £¼ (B + S - R). Insufficient capital coverage tightens capital-chain conditions and the industry enters an adjustment phase.

### 3.6 Recession Pathways for Two Capital Categories

1. Financial Capital: Deteriorating expectations ¡ú financing contraction ¡ú valuation inversion ¡ú rapid market clearing. Fast recession speed.
2. Industrial Capital: Sustained losses ¡ú parent-company pressure ¡ú budget downgrade ¡ú business contraction. Slow and protracted recession rhythm.

### 3.7 Early-Warning Value of Latent Variable S

High-level position cashing-out takes place during periods of maximum market optimism and hype and constitutes a leading top-cycle signal. It cannot be fully quantified from public statistics; behavioral signals take priority over numerical metrics.

### 3.8 Ultimate Conclusions for This Chapter

1. The AI-industry capital inflection point originates from failure of capital underpinning rather than short-term profit fluctuations.
2. The sole survival criterion for the industry: whether new financial capital inflow can cover comprehensive net industrial gaps.
3. Financial capital retreats first; industrial capital contracts with a lag.
4. As long as external end-user revenue cannot cover industrial consumption, industry prosperity remains capital-constructed non-steady-state pseudo-prosperity.

### 3.9 Constraints for This Chapter

1. Industry cycles shall not be judged by isolated financing volumes, market enthusiasm or technical iterations.
2. Strictly distinguish operational closed-loops from capital-arbitrage pseudo-closed-loops.
3. All system-wide cyclical deductions must rely upon the four-variable capital-gap model.

## Chapter 4 Summary of Observable External Phenomena for Dual Inflection Points

### 4.1 Phenomena Indicating Arrival of the End-User-Demand Inflection Point

1. C-end: Rising share of paid revenue, growth driven by incremental scenarios, fading subsidy dependence.
2. B-end: POC pilots convert into regular operational procurement; AI is deeply embedded within core industrial workflows.
3. Endogenous dimension: Sustained computing-power procurement by incumbents; improved manpower efficiency and profit margins for core businesses; internalized AI-driven value.
4. Industry-wide: Reduced capital dependence; order generation shifts from capital-fueled spending toward end-user-demand transmission; valuation logic pivots toward cash-flow and real-revenue metrics.

### 4.2 Capital-Inflection-Point Destabilization and Avalanche-Style Phenomena

#### 4.2.1 Early-Stage Destabilization Phase

Extended financing cycles, fewer new projects, difficult existing-share exits, reduced market sensitivity toward positive news; decelerating growth in AI investment by large enterprises, compression of non-core businesses; slowing growth in computing-power orders; internal industry optimism contrasted with cool external sentiment; rising high-level position-cashing-out activity.

#### 4.2.2 Late-Stage Avalanche Phase

Frozen primary-market financing, shrinking transaction volumes, rising project liquidations and M&A activity; AI-segment losses drag down parent-company financial statements; contracting industry-wide computing-power procurement, team lay-offs and project shutdowns; complete reversal of market expectations and onset of systemic industry contraction.

### 4.3 Core Observation Constraints
